4.1 Introduction to Magnets
Reshma lives in a coastal town of Kerala and is very fond of writing short stories. She was writing a new story about a ship carrying spices wherein the sailors got caught in a storm with an overcast sky and stars were not visible. She learnt that travellers used a device, known as a magnetic compass, for finding directions. Reshma had seen pencil boxes and purses which had magnets to keep them closed. A writing board in her school also had a duster with a magnet.

The magnets used by sailors in the olden days were based on naturally occurring magnets, known as lodestones. Lodestones were discovered in ancient Greece. It is said that a shepherd named Magnes used to take his herd of sheep and goats to the nearby mountains for grazing. He had a wooden stick with a small iron cap at one end. One day he found that his stick was being pulled by a rock. This rock was a natural magnet and it pulled the iron cap of the shepherd's stick. Such rocks were named 'Magnetite'.
